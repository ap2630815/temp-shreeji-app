BILINGUAL TEXT RULES (English + Gujarati) - standard for every screen
==============================================================================
Code: macros and the rules at the top of app/templates/partials/bilabel.html; styles in app/templates/base.html.

1. Order and coverage
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- English always comes first, Gujarati second.
- Every visible text has both. Exceptions: numbers, dates, names, codes.

2. Placement by element type
----------------------------------------------------------------------
STACKED (English above, Gujarati below, left-aligned):
  buttons, tabs, table headers, badges, summary-tile titles, card headings, labels, page titles (h1; Gujarati slightly smaller)
SIDE BY SIDE:
  sentences, table row labels, notes, field help, errors, flash messages, dashboard cards
A Gujarati value under an English name in a table (e.g. a farmer's Gujarati name): stacked, as a small sub-line.

3. Side by side = English, 2 spaces, Gujarati
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Use t(en, gu) or bi(key). The source holds one space; the rule  .bi > .gu { margin-left: .3em }  in base.html adds the second.
  Stacked pairs inside buttons, badges, notices, .bi-stack and the h1 of .stackbi pages are not affected.
- Hand-written side-by-side text: class "gu gu-side" on the Gujarati span (same 2-space gap).
  Applied in: dashboard cards and master cards, form field help and errors, analysis notes and titles, flash messages,
  the purchase-group form error.
- Decided exceptions, left exactly as they are:
    "All  બધા" dropdown options in layout.html (2 &nbsp;)
    "All villages  બધા ગામ" in partials/purchase_group_form.html
    "Date:  <date>" in partials/statement_body.html and partials/industry_body.html
    the "- " before the Gujarati in form labels (.lf-grid in base.html)
    the form-page rules (.gu-same in base.html)

4. Stacked
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- stack(en, gu)         labels, tabs, titles (Business year label, tabs, Payables / Analysis titles)
- th(en, gu)            table headers
- heading(en, gu)       card headings
- stat(...) + sub(...)  summary tiles and the lines under them
- Buttons and badges stack automatically (.btn .bi, .badge .bi).
- The Gujarati line is smaller than the English: about .8em in buttons and badges, text-xs / text-sm elsewhere.

5. Gujarati colour
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Gujarati text has 80% opacity (.gu in base.html).
- Pages inside .gu-same (New / Edit forms) keep their own rule: same size and colour as the English; table cells and buttons
  on those pages keep the small, dull Gujarati.

6. How to write them
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Never write <span class="gu ..."> by hand when one of the helpers above fits.
- A label used in more than 2 places goes into app/core/labels.py and is used as L[key] / bi(key).
- Adding a screen: pick the helper from section 3 or 4. No new CSS.
- Already on the helpers: Business year label (all list pages), tab labels, Payables / Analysis titles,
  Save / Back / Edit / Statement / No-records buttons (bi(key)), PDF buttons, summary tiles on /Trips/<id> and /PurchaseGroup/<id>.

7. Dashboard cards
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Every card is as wide as the widest card's content plus two spaces (measured by a script in dashboard.html); cards wrap into rows
  starting from the left.

8. Known gaps (hand-written pairs, about 84, left to keep their look)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- sidebar items and top bar (layout.html)
- printed statements and receipts (partials/*_body.html)
- farmer-name sub-lines in tables
- the form pages

9. Gujarati filled from the English (forms with both)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Every form that has an English box and a Gujarati twin (name / name_gu, village_name / village_name_gu, farmer_name__1..4 / farmer_name_gu__1..4 ...)
  fills the Gujarati by itself while the English is typed or picked. The Gujarati box stays editable: once you type in it, that is a correction and it is
  kept - changing the English afterwards does NOT overwrite it (an Edit form whose saved Gujarati differs from what the English would give is treated the
  same). Clearing the English clears the Gujarati and starts again (auto-fill is back for the next entry).
- Built in and offline (app/static/translit.js, loaded by base.html); nothing leaves the server. The forms stay server-rendered - no form was converted
  to a dynamic page; a small script reads the field names (X and X_gu, or X__n and X_gu__n).
- How the Gujarati is made: whole words from the word list GU_WORDS first (grains are translated: Wheat -> ઘઉં; surnames, first names and villages are
  spelled), then endings (bhai, ben, kumar, lal, ji ...), then the sound rules. It is a good first draft, not perfect (long and short vowels cannot be
  known from the English), so check the Gujarati. To fix a word for good, add it to GU_WORDS (lower-case key).
- Rule for new forms: name the Gujarati field <English field>_gu and nothing else is needed.
